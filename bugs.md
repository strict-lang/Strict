- A `for` comprehension whose body calls GPU work is about 20x slower than the same calls in a sequential loop: in StrictComfy's H3ClipEncoder, `for states` returning `norm.Affine(value)` (GroupNorm affines of 5 frame maps of 256x256x128 from conv outputs) took 290-370 ms, while the same five Affine calls in a `for` with a mutable sum took 14 ms. GPU utilization was about 10 % and nsys CPU sampling was mostly kernel time and host copies, as if the comprehension evaluated the deferred conv outputs on host threads. The H3 video VAE encode of 22 frames went from 97 s to 9.7 s by computing the affines in a sequential loop (StrictComfy, 2026-11-15).
- Negative zero differs: `-zero` of a variable holding 0 gives +0 on the VM (`1 / -zero` is Infinity) while IEEE negation keeps -0, and the literal `-0` became +0 in native tests (constant folding prints it through Text); decide whether Strict has -0 and make VM, native and folding agree (2026-11-15).

- H3Video at 704x1280 with --length 396 (104303 tokens, 56 heads of 128) does not fit the 4090: at the first attention the pool has no free block and the driver 2 GB, the attention result needs 2852 MB (32 bit), and nothing can be evicted (the weights stream from locked host memory, only 6 to 10 device copies are cached); the rest of the 24 GB are activations kept on the GPU, about 3 GB each at 32 bit (input, norm, queries, keys, values). 576x1024 (67k tokens, 1.9 GB per activation) fits. It now ends with `AttentionNeedsGpu: 104303 tokens, 104303 positions and 56 heads did not fit into the free GPU memory` instead of a 4 TB CPU allocation. Making it fit needs one of: 16 bit activations and attention results (half the memory, precision checked against the reference), activations spilled to host memory when the GPU is short, or H3 running a block in token chunks (2026-11-16).
