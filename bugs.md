# Bugs

- GC race: a young collection inside a pool job (more than 2 threads) frees or misses an object a job thread still uses. Repro without a swap (2026-11-23): build the seed of `Compiler\Bootstrap.cmd` with -O3 into obj (its mlir-opt, mlir-translate and clang lines with outputs in obj/seedO3, not its last build step), then `STRICT_GPU=0 obj/seedO3/NativeCompiler.exe test Strict.strict . native` from the Strict folder: it crashes 2-6 s in, right after a "young, pool job" collection (AccessViolation in KnownTypes.Index, TypeInference.TypeOf, TypeEntry.ShortName ...) in about 1 of 3 runs, only when the .strictbinary files carry another executable's stamp (everything compiles again; alternate two seed builds to get that every run). Not the early freeing of replaced values: STRICT_RC_DEAD=0 still crashed 1 of 6, a seed without the small dispose or without the large page return crashed too; single runs prove nothing at this rate. The SLP vectorizer is the trigger: 7 fresh runs each, -O3 crashed 4, -O3 -fno-vectorize 4, -O3 -fno-slp-vectorize 0 (obj/seedVariants). In the runtime it merges the (object, size) pairs of the mark stacks (strict_gc_drain_worker, local_donate, local_take, push) into vector copies and the two first words of swept blocks into one 16 byte store (strict_gc_sweep_page, take_pages): look there for a plain store another thread reads without an atomic (a merged or moved store), or a miscompile. Earlier, STRICT_THREADS=1 or 2, STRICT_YOUNG=0 and -O2 hid it too (not repeated at this rate). Normal -O3 builds (thin LTO in 64 parts) have not hit it (21-42 young pool-job collections per check, clean self-builds). Theory (unverified): the single-module seed inlines the runtime deeply and keeps only a derived pointer (an element or end pointer past the object) while a job thread is parked, so the conservative stack scan misses the object. Bootstrap.cmd links with -O2 until it is fixed; release builds use -O3.
