4. **GPU memory at the edge of the WDDM budget**: lists on the GPU are freed by their counts at a missing block (strict_rc_reclaim, device flag 64), so MossImage at 720x1280 runs without full collections per step (95 % GPU busy, 470 ms); with ~0.9 GB used by other programs the pool sits at its 256 MB headroom and still has 1-8 full collections, 2-5 cuMemAlloc/cuMemFree/cuCtxSynchronize and ~100 young collections per step (462-499 ms, was 1.5 s): a sub-allocating pool (any free block serves any size) would end that; measured on C:/StrictDumps/moss720 (8 GB peak, 240 ms steps) with another process holding 15.8 GB: steps take 320-485 ms with one 7 MB allocation and one young collection per step (printf probe in strict_gpu_alloc/free/strict_gc_run), so there it is WDDM paging at the edge of the budget, not the pool; the VAE decode after the steps allocates 5.7 GB in 68 blocks and frees 214 (446 ms), 6.4 instead of 1.2 s (14.5 GB held: no slowdown); the arena wrap synchronizes every second step; MossImage's per-step reference list (7 MB) is only freed by a later collection.
5. **Generated 32 bit GPU loops still cost 2-3x hand written kernels**: on the RTX 3080 they are instruction bound (ncu 2026-10-23: Rotated, Activated, Modulated and Columns 120-330 instructions per number at 72-84 % SM against 36-78 % DRAM throughput). MossBlock_Rotated took 279 us (exact kernel, 3740x3072 bfloat16) against 121 us for the same math in CUDA with checked loads (kb harness: the real PTX launched on synthetic data, scratchpad of the 2026-10-22 session). Measured by removing pieces of its PTX: the uniform format branch of every read (strict_device_floats, 32 or 16 bit) costs 69 us; doing the format without a branch (both loads, one aligned load, predicated loads) or the bounds checks with selects measured slower. It needs the formats known when the kernel is compiled: a launch-time specialization (formats written into the PTX of a kernel at its first launch with them, cached per signature) would remove it. Divisions: exact kernels assume their divisor parameters in [1, 2^31] (MlirPassed.Bounded, the host runs them only when those have a magic number, guards bit 32 + index) and grouped kernels their %at below %count below 2^32, so LLVM drops the sign, size and divisor checks and the slow path of a loop index numerator and shares one quotient between / and % (Columns 147 -> 125 us, Activated 643 -> 547 us, MossImage mix720 steps 8 ms faster); left: numerators that are products (Rotated keeps the slow path of head * size / width) and the floor correction.
6. **BenchBlur on one thread**: clang 23 C (`-O3 -march=native`) takes 195 ms, Strict 235-248 (2026-10-23, 24 threads 26 against OpenMP 53, GPU 7.7 warm); compare their loops (C vectorizes the hoisted check, Strict calls the whole function per pixel with its guards).
7. **Trees as pools with 32 bit backward offsets** (from [Goose](https://github.com/aardappel/goose), its `tree` benchmark: 12 byte nodes, 1.1x Rust arenas, 4x `Box`/`unique_ptr`): an immutable value is always built after its members, so a type whose members hold instances of itself (`SyntaxNode.children`, every parse of every build) can keep all its instances in one grow-only block and link them as small backward u32 offsets, no allocation, count or pointer per node and one free for the whole tree.
